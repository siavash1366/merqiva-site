MARITIME BUYING SIGNALS

Not every maritime event is a sales opportunity. The signal only matters when it fits what you sell.

Merqiva treats market signals as inputs, not conclusions. A new vessel, contract, drydock or management change becomes commercially useful only after product fit, account relevance, evidence quality, buyer context and timing are evaluated.

High-value signal families

Signals that can change supplier priority.

The relevance of each signal depends on the product category and available evidence. Merqiva does not claim that a signal by itself proves purchase intent.

New vessel acquisition / newbuildCan create equipment, commissioning, integration or service hypotheses.
Fleet expansionMay increase equipment, maintenance or service demand for relevant suppliers.
Contract or charter awardCan change operating intensity, vessel deployment or project requirements.
Drydock / refit / retrofitCan create timing windows for technical and equipment categories.
Regulatory / class / compliance changeCan affect safety, communications, environmental and technical requirements.
Ownership or management changeCan alter procurement responsibility, fleet strategy or supplier relationships.
Technical or procurement leadership changeCan indicate a new buyer path, but should not be treated as purchase intent alone.
Port, yard or offshore expansionCan create downstream supplier hypotheses that still require account-level qualification.
Merqiva standard

Signal ≠ opportunity.

An opportunity should survive four additional questions: does the account fit, does the product fit, is the evidence good enough, and is there a credible buyer/action path?

Verified fact

A material claim supported by an identified source where available.

Inference

An analytical conclusion that should be clearly labeled rather than presented as fact.

Unknown

Information that has not been verified and should remain explicitly unresolved.
